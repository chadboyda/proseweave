Stop scrolling. This might be the most important thing you read today.

My 6-year-old asked me a question at breakfast that completely changed how I think about work.

She was eating her cereal, looked up at me and said, "Daddy, why do you look at your phone more than you look at me?"

I didn't have an answer. I still don't.

I've been building my company for eight years. We've raised money. We've hired 60 people. We've been featured in all the places you're supposed to be featured in. And my daughter thinks my phone is more interesting than she is.

Let that sink in for a second.

We spend our whole lives chasing metrics. Revenue. Followers. Titles. Promotions. And the people who matter most get the leftover version of us. The tired version. The distracted version. The "hang on, one more email" version.

So I made a decision that day. And I want to share it with you because I think a lot of you need to hear it.

Phone goes in a drawer from 6pm to 8pm. No exceptions. Not for investors. Not for customers. Not for "urgent" things that are never actually urgent.

Weekends are for family first. If the business can't survive me being offline on a Saturday, I haven't built a business. I've built a trap.

I tell my team to do the same. And guess what? Productivity went UP. Burnout went DOWN. People started actually enjoying their jobs again.

Here's what nobody tells you about success. Nobody on their deathbed wishes they answered more Slack messages. Nobody. Ever.

Your kids won't remember your quarterly numbers. They'll remember if you were there.

I'm not perfect. I still slip up. Last week I caught myself checking email at the dinner table and my daughter gave me The Look. But I'm trying. And trying is where it starts.

So here's my challenge to you. Tonight, put your phone in another room for two hours. Just two hours. See what happens.

Then come back and tell me how it went.
