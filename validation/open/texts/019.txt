Unpopular opinion: most meetings should be emails.

I know, I know. You've heard it before. But hear me out, because I think we're still getting this wrong in 2024.

Last month I tracked every meeting I attended. Every single one.

The results shocked me.

62 meetings. 41 hours. And do you know how many of them actually needed to be a meeting?

Seven.

SEVEN.

The rest? Status updates that could have been a two-line message. "Syncs" where nobody synced anything. "Quick chats" that were neither quick nor chats. Brainstorms where the loudest person won and everyone else checked their phones.

That's 30+ hours I'll never get back. That's almost a full work week. Gone.

Now multiply that by every person on your team. Multiply it by every week of the year. That's not a productivity problem. That's a culture problem.

So here's what I did about it.

I declined every recurring meeting without an agenda. Every one. Politely, but firmly. "Happy to join once there's an agenda!"

I started sending async updates on Friday instead of doing a Monday status meeting. Engagement actually went up because people could read it when it suited them.

I blocked two mornings a week for deep work. No meetings allowed. My calendar looks empty on those days. It isn't. That's where the real work happens.

The result? I shipped more in the last four weeks than in the previous three months.

The pushback was real at first. Some people thought I was being difficult. One person told me I was "not a team player." But after a few weeks, three other people on my team started doing the same thing.

Here's the truth nobody wants to admit. Meetings feel like work. They look like work on a calendar. But feeling busy is not the same as being productive.

Busy is a choice. So is focus.

The best leaders I know protect their team's time like it's the most valuable resource in the company. Because it is.

Your calendar is a reflection of your priorities. What does yours say about you?
