I got rejected from 47 jobs last year.

Forty. Seven.

I'm not saying this for sympathy. I'm saying it because nobody talks about this part.

Everyone posts the "I'm thrilled to announce" updates. Nobody posts the 11pm refresh of an inbox that stays empty. Nobody posts the "we've decided to move forward with other candidates" email that arrives three minutes after a final round you thought you crushed.

So here's what 47 rejections taught me. Read it twice.

Rejection is data, not a verdict. Every no told me something. Sometimes it told me my resume was buried under keywords. Sometimes it told me I was interviewing like a candidate instead of a partner. Sometimes it told me nothing, and that taught me something too.

Your network is your net worth. Out of 47 applications, zero came through a referral. The job I finally landed? A former coworker sent one message. One. That's it. Let that sink in.

Confidence is a skill, not a feeling. I didn't feel confident in interview 48. I acted confident. There's a difference, and it changed everything.

The right door only opens after the wrong ones close. I used to think every rejection was a door slammed in my face. Now I think they were doors I was never supposed to walk through.

Here's the thing most people won't tell you. The job search isn't about being the most qualified person in the room. It's about being the most memorable. Qualifications get you the interview. Story gets you the offer.

I'm not special. I'm not lucky. I just refused to quit at 46.

If you're in the middle of your own 47 right now, I see you. Keep going. Your yes is coming.

And if you're a hiring manager reading this: please, send the rejection email. Silence is worse.
